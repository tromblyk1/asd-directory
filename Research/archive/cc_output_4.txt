  The service-mix tradeoff, stated plainly: the data supports two services at credible depth, not five. I chose 15 aba +
   4 life-skills + 1 ados-testing over a cosmetically varied set because the alternative is padding with 5-verified
  combos to manufacture variety. A pilot exists to answer "does this format rank at all" — 20 thin pages answer that
  question wrong and can't be un-indexed cheaply. The templating risk is real but it lives in the copy, not the service
  count: 15 ABA pages differentiated only by a swapped city name will read as generated regardless of how many services
  sit beside them. Spend the effort on per-city substance (actual local provider names, county scholarship notes, real
  travel distances) rather than on service spread.

  City coverage: 15 distinct cities. Pages 16-20 deliberately double up on Tampa, Miami, Jacksonville, St. Pete and
  Orlando rather than reaching into new cities, which buys a read on whether two service pages in one city cannibalize
  each other — useful signal before scaling to ~700. If you'd rather maximize distinct-city coverage, swap 18/19 for aba
   Hialeah (15) and aba Hollywood (15) and accept a 17-aba/2-life-skills/1-ados split.

  cityCoordinates.json flags

  All 15 proposed cities have entries, but three problems block the nearby-city module regardless:

  1. Nothing consumes the file. No .ts/.tsx in src/frontend references cityCoordinates — only the planning docs and
  _archive/scripts/generate_city_coordinates.js. The module is unbuilt, not merely unwired.
  2. Key format is inconsistent across the 286 keys — TAMPA and MIAMI are upper-case, Tallahassee, Orlando, Davie, Palm
  Bay are title-case. Any lookup must normalize.
  3. ST. PETERSBURG will miss on exact match. The JSON key is ST PETERSBURG, no period; canonical_city is ST. PETERSBURG
   with one. That breaks two proposed pages (#11, #19). Port St. Lucie has the same shape mismatch and is in the cohort
  at #20 raw.
